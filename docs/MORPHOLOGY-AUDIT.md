# Definition pipeline audit and correction

Audit date: 2026-10-30. Sol coordinated three GPT-6 Luna workers. Changes are local and have not been deployed.

## Established cause

`src/adapters/greek/generated/koine-core.ts` stores the lemma ἀγαπάω with the citation gloss “I love”. `src/adapters/greek/morphgnt.ts::decodeMorphGnt` separately decodes person, tense, voice, mood and number; `src/adapters/greek/analyze.ts` attaches that morphology and the lemma gloss to candidates. The evidence inspected does not establish a faulty future-person decoder.

`src/golearn/queries.ts::tokenSupport` previously selected `lemma.gloss` for the reader, while `src/ui/reader/TokenCard.tsx` rendered it alongside separate morphology. The extension/Biblia path similarly copied candidate glosses in `packages/golearn-extension/src/parser/index.ts::parseSelection` and prioritized them in `packages/golearn-extension/src/popup/card.ts::createCardUI`. Lexical and inflected meanings shared one field. This explains the reported wording without assuming that the morphology itself was lost.

Biblia's `packages/biblia/src/app.ts` supplies sentence text and a source reading hint. The hint can prioritize an existing matching candidate; it does not create an analysis. The change also carries the verse reference into the parser.

## Implemented boundaries

`src/core/word-analysis.ts` supplies a shared evidence projection separating lexical, inflected and contextual meanings, normalized surface, language, grammatical features, sentence, references, confidence and ambiguity. Missing evidence remains null. Reader and extension models expose the projection; the UIs distinguish lexical-only fallback from a supported inflected translation and show the literal meaning beneath a contextual rendering.

The initial patch used four spelling-specific translations and added too much detail to the immediate card. Following the owner's review, `src/adapters/greek/word-analysis.ts` instead realizes English from the analyzer's finite active indicative present/future features, with person and number. A strict simple citation gloss supplies the English verb; explicit English lexical entries handle forms such as have/has. Greek paradigms are not invented or regenerated. Compound or unclear supplied glosses remain unsupported. “You shall love” still requires the explicit GAL.5.14 reference and compatible inflected evidence; that contextual rule is limited to this cited passage. Existing lemma data is not rewritten.

The immediate card now contains the primary meaning, a source line and compact lemma meaning. Literal, contextual-reference and alternative-reading details belong in the existing deeper panels. Equivalent morphological candidates no longer generate a false ambiguity notice. This revision improves the supported grammatical realization, but does not complete the original multilingual contextual-translation objective.

GoReview changes and preservation evidence are detailed in [MORPHOLOGY-REVIEW-AUDIT.md](MORPHOLOGY-REVIEW-AUDIT.md). Form answers no longer inherit lemma glosses indiscriminately, and warm facts include variety in their cache key. No persisted scheduling/event migration is performed.

## Verb forms in every language (2026-11-03)

The owner, 2026-11-03: verbs were parsed correctly but often translated in the wrong person ("2nd person is often translated as 1st"). The cause was the citation: a Greek lexicon cites a verb in the first person ("I say, speak"), and the realization of 2026-10-30 accepted only a one-word "I love" citation in the present or future active indicative, so 15 of the 128 verbs of the compiled Koine core could be realized and every other form (every aorist, imperfect, perfect, subjunctive, imperative, participle, every middle or passive, every multi-sense citation) showed the first-person citation under the word. Hebrew and Latin realized nothing.

`src/core/english-verb.ts` now writes English from a citation and a neutral shape (person, number, gender, time, mood, voice, a prefix, a stem nuance): the citation's first usable sense (first-person, infinitive or bare citations; Whitaker's "(PASS)" sense for a passive; "/" and "," and ";" as sense separators), inflected with an irregular-verb table and the regular rules, every person with "he, she, or it" where the gender is unknown and "you (plural)" for a plural second person. The adapters map their own features to that shape: `src/adapters/greek/word-analysis.ts` (present, imperfect "was …ing", future, aorist "…ed", perfect "has …ed", pluperfect; subjunctive "may", optative "might", imperative "…!"; infinitives and participles by tense and voice; a deponent in -μαι keeps its sense in the middle and passive, εἰμί and οἶδα keep their own forms), `src/adapters/hebrew/word-analysis.ts` (qatal, yiqtol, wayyiqtol "and he …", weqatal, imperative, cohortative, jussive, participles, infinitives; the binyan written literally only where the citation is the Qal sense: listed roots for the Hifil/Hofal causative and the Hitpael reflexive, listed Nifal-only roots kept active, a stative first sense never made passive, a derived stem taking the next sense of a citation that opens with a state), `src/adapters/latin/word-analysis.ts` (the six tenses, the subjunctive's four, imperatives, infinitives, participles, gerund, gerundive, supine; deponents already active), and `src/adapters/word-analysis.ts` for every other language from plain UD features (indicative tenses and imperatives only). `candidateWordAnalysis` (src/core/word-analysis.ts) builds the projection for every reading: a list-valued feature (Latin venit, Tense=Perf,Pres) becomes two readings, the first shown and the rest as other possible readings, realized where they can be.

The extension parser, Biblia's reader (`src/golearn/queries.ts`) and the GoReview facts provider use these; a Greek review answer accepts "he said", "she said" and "it said" beside "He, she, or it said" (`answerVariants`). The lemma meaning beside a realized form is the citation as an infinitive ("to say, speak").

Limits: the first sense of a citation is the one realized, and Strong's orders senses by the root idea, so a verb used in another sense shows that sense (ἐβαπτίσθη "was dipped": the lemma meaning "to dip, submerge, baptize" stays beside it); a Nifal, Hifil or Hitpael of a root outside the lists takes the citation as it stands; the other languages' dictionary-only glosses (the Wiktionary senses the card fetches) are not realized. Tests: test/core/english-verb.test.ts, test/adapters/{greek,hebrew,latin}-meanings.test.ts over the compiled lexica, test/extension/hebrew-latin-word-analysis.test.ts and the Biblia card test for וַיֹּאמֶר.

## Hebrew glosses and morphology (2026-11-05)

The owner, Isaiah 1:2: וְרוֹמַמְתִּי read "I exalteded". The source data was right (Open Scriptures: Polel perfect 1cs with וְ; lexicon "be high, exalted, rise"). The cause was the realizer: it treated each comma-separated piece of a citation as a base verb. "exalted" stands for "be exalted", the Polel skipped the stative first sense and took it as its verb, and the regular rule added -ed. Across the Tanakh index the same fault wrote 344 distinct malformed verb forms, plus forms built on adjectives and nouns ("He badded" was the same path, "much", "weighty").

- `src/core/english-verb.ts` classifies the word that opens a sense before anything is added to it: a base verb, a past participle and its verb ("exalted": exalt), a present participle ("fearing": fear), or a word that is not a verb. A state with its "be" left out is read as such ("be high, exalted" is "be high", "be exalted"), an adverb is passed over, and `realizeEnglishVerb` refuses a head that is not a base verb and then checks its own output (`isWellFormedEnglishVerb`). The lists were read off every verb citation in the Tanakh index.
- `src/adapters/hebrew/word-analysis.ts` keeps the three meanings apart and chooses the sense from the stem: a listed root and stem has its own English (רום Polel "raise", יצא Hifil "bring out"); an active stem takes the first action, or makes a state ("made heavy"); a passive stem takes the citation's participle ("was exalted"); the Aramaic Hitpeel and Hitpaal are passives. A conjunction or an object suffix in the source's parts is said: "And I raised", "I brought you out".
- `src/adapters/hebrew/contextual.ts` is a short authored list of contextual renderings with reasons, each bound to its verse. Nothing is computed; a word not listed has no contextual meaning.
- `src/adapters/hebrew/form-analysis.ts` and the card's "analysis" section give the root, the components, and what the stem, conjugation and agreement contribute. The verb's preformative and ending are cut only where the features name them and the letters show them.
- "No dictionary service is connected" was the card's message for a host that never asks for an entry, which is Biblia. It described the host, not the data. Biblia now fills the forms and structure from its analyser, and says plainly that it carries no full dictionary entries. The extension asks its pack; the Hebrew pack is experimental and has no dictionary, so it answers that no dictionary pack is installed. The forms of a Hebrew lemma are attested in the Tanakh index, and the card now says so instead of "the rules only".

Measured over the 73,667 verb tokens of the index: every form written is well formed, and 99.8% have an English of their own; the rest are citations with no verb in them (nouns, adverbs), which show the lemma meaning and a note. Limits: the qatal and yiqtol defaults are defaults; a sense is the citation's or the stem table's first, not the one the sentence needs; only the verses in `contextual.ts` have a contextual rendering, because this repository holds no word-level English of the Tanakh; and `src/golearn/queries.ts` passes no morpheme parts, so that older path does not say a conjunction. Tests: test/adapters/hebrew-regression.test.ts, test/adapters/hebrew-tanakh-sweep.test.ts (skipped where the index is not installed), test/biblia/app.test.ts.

## Remaining work and limits

- General inflected English realization and contextual disambiguation for Greek and the other languages remain incomplete. Greek realization currently covers finite active indicative present/future readings with usable lexical evidence. Other tense, aspect, voice, mood and unclear lexical evidence remain unsupported; this does not certify linguistic accuracy outside those bounds.
- The language inventory and limitations are in [MORPHOLOGY-COVERAGE-AUDIT.md](MORPHOLOGY-COVERAGE-AUDIT.md). Projection tests across languages prove separation of fields, not translation accuracy. Equivalent complete reader-to-review translation tests for every installed language remain outstanding.
- Existing stored learner events, lexical records, card identities and scheduling histories are preserved by leaving them untouched. Persisted historical exercise snapshots and all external deployment caches have not been migrated or comprehensively audited. Recomputed facts apply the correction; this is not proof that every previously saved display is repaired.
- The DOM regression exercises real compiled Greek data through the parser and popup, including Unicode normalization. This does not substitute for a manual installed-extension/device run or the full Biblical corpus; existing Biblia corpus-dependent tests skip when their data files are absent.
- No network/model fallback was added. The new projection and small lookup are local. There is no new performance benchmark or specialist-reviewed multilingual gold corpus.
