## Ad mare
Heri Anna et Lucius ad mare venerunt. Samuel et Marcus quoque venerunt. || Yesterday Anna and Lucius came to the sea. Samuel and Marcus came too.
Mare non longe est. Postquam ad litus venerunt, mare viderunt. || The sea is not far. After they came to the shore, they saw the sea.

[nico] Ecce mare! Ecce aqua magna! || Look, the sea! Look, the big water!
[luis] Mare est. Ecce litus. Hic ambulamus. || It is the sea. Look, the shore. Here we walk.
[sam] Quid est hoc in aqua? Estne insula? || What is this in the water? Is it an island?
[ana] Insula est, et longe est. Ecce, navis ad insulam it! || It is an island, and it is far away. Look, a ship goes to the island!
[nico] Navem video! Navis magna est. In nave multi homines sunt. || I see the ship! The ship is big. In the ship there are many people.
[luis] Homines ad insulam eunt. Ibi habitant. || The people go to the island. They live there.

Marcus in litore sedet. Aquam videt. Piscem in aqua videt. || Marcus sits on the shore. He sees the water. He sees a fish in the water.

[nico] Ecce piscis! Piscem in aqua video! || Look, a fish! I see a fish in the water!
[sam] Ubi est? Non video. || Where is it? I do not see it.
[nico] Ibi est! Statim video. Sed nunc piscis in aqua non est. || It is there! I see it at once. But now the fish is not in the water.
[ana] Piscis aquam amat. Ego aquam amo. || The fish loves the water. I love water.

Anna panem et pomum in litore parat, et omnes edunt et bibunt. || Anna prepares bread and fruit on the shore, and all eat and drink.
Lucius dixit: Heri in schola fui. Hodie in schola non sum. Hodie hic sum. Bene est! || Lucius said: Yesterday I was at school. Today I am not at school. Today I am here. It is well!
Samuel dixit: Dies bonus est. Gratias Deo ago. || Samuel said: It is a good day. I give thanks to God.

Post cenam ad urbem eunt. Marcus iterum aquam videt. || After the meal they go to the town. Marcus looks at the water again.

[nico] Cras iterum ad mare venimus? || Do we come to the sea again tomorrow?
[ana] Cras non venimus. Cras in valetudinario laboro. || Tomorrow we do not come. Tomorrow I work at the clinic.
[luis] Cras in schola sum. Sed dies Dominica venimus. || Tomorrow I am at school. But on Sunday we come.
[nico] Bene! Dies Dominica iterum venimus. || Good! On Sunday we come again.
[sam] Gratias vobis ago, amici. Hodie dies bonus fuit. Pax vobis! || Thank you, friends. Today was a good day. Peace to you!

Canis quoque ad litus venit. Marcus canem amat. Canis aquam amat, et in aqua ambulat. || The dog comes to the shore too. Marcus loves the dog. The dog loves water, and walks in the water.
Anna et Samuel ad montem ambulant. Ex monte mare vident, et insulam vident. Avis in arbore cantat. || Anna and Samuel walk to the hill. From the hill they see the sea, and they see the island. A bird sings in the tree.
