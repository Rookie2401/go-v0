## What each comparison can and cannot show

- **missing (the dictionary has no entry).** Can show: the dictionary file has no entry the mapper could read for that written form. Cannot show: that the hand entry is at fault, or that the word is not Thai; Wiktionary may list the word under another spelling or lack it.
- **part of speech.** Can show: the hand part of speech and the dictionary's share no kind of word even under the equivalence table below. Cannot show which is right; the two tag sets divide Thai words differently, and Wiktionary lines whose part of speech the mapper does not read are not counted.
- **meaning.** Can show: no content word of the hand gloss turns up in any gloss of the form. Cannot show: that the meanings differ. A synonym, a paraphrase or a narrower sense looks the same to this test. The dictionary side is the capped short view the mapper keeps (the first gloss of each sense).
- **register.** Can show: a register tag on one side that the other side's labels lack or differ from. Cannot show a mistake: Wiktionary labels sparingly and labels single senses, so an unlabelled entry is weak evidence. Only labels the dictionary actually uses are compared; the course's royal, religious, regional and child-directed tags have no counterpart there.
- **classifier.** Can show: the dictionary neither files the form as a classifier nor lists it as a classifier of any noun. Cannot show that it is not used as one.
- **tone (exception and settled lists).** Can show: the tones the engine reads from a course respelling differ from every listed IPA reading with the same number of syllables. Cannot show which reading is right, and says nothing about words outside the two exception lists.
- **syllables (exception and settled lists).** Can show: the syllable count of a course respelling matches no listed reading. Cannot show which division is right.
- **pronunciation (respelling and hand romanization).** Can show: a course respelling or romanization that is written differently from every listed one. Different systems and conventions produce such differences with identical sound; a romanization is compared only through a coarse skeleton that ignores tone.

All of them compare the hand data with the dictionary as the mapper reads it. A line the mapper skips, a gloss it truncates or a label it does not map is invisible here.

## Method notes

Part-of-speech equivalence used (hand part of speech: dictionary parts of speech accepted as the same kind of word):

- NOUN: NOUN, PROPN, CLF
- PROPN: PROPN, NOUN
- VERB: VERB, AUX, ADJ
- AUX: AUX, VERB, ADV, PART
- ADJ: ADJ, VERB, ADV, DET, NUM
- ADV: ADV, ADJ, PART, ADP, CONJ, DET, VERB
- PRON: PRON, NOUN, DET
- DET: DET, ADJ, PRON, NUM, ADV
- ADP: ADP, CONJ, NOUN, VERB, ADV
- CONJ: CONJ, SCONJ, ADP, ADV, PART
- SCONJ: SCONJ, CONJ, PRON, ADP, NOUN, PART
- PART: PART, ADV, INTJ, CONJ, AUX
- NUM: NUM, ADJ, DET, NOUN
- INTJ: INTJ, PART, VERB
- CLF: CLF, NOUN

Register labels the dictionary uses often enough (at least 5 entries) to be compared: archaic, child-directed, colloquial, formal, historical, literary, slang. Strong labels checked in the other direction: slang, archaic, royal, vulgar, offensive, derogatory.
