# GoReview 3 orchestration and evidence

This describes the implemented GoReview/GoPractice planner and the Repository projection it reads. It is a behavior note for the current source, not a claim that the provisional heuristics are calibrated. The Repository knowledge projection version is **`projection@7`**. FSRS schedules and Repository knowledge are separate models.

## Eligibility and evidence

Automatic planning is gated by shared review-evidence selection, card lifecycle, learner controls, exercise facts, due time, knowledge, and budgets. Conflicting or withdrawn evidence branches do not create a weakness or justify a new automatic card. Invalid-question, ambiguous, unable-to-grade, challenged, audio-failed, assisted, hinted, answer-shown, and non-retrieval attempts do not become remediation failures. Unsupported historical scheduler profiles suspend affected scheduling rather than silently replaying them with different parameters.

For an existing card, an effective due obligation remains due even if its unit is now marked Automatic. A not-due card needs an explicit request for early practice. Automatic admissions require observed knowledge beyond New and must pass the Seen evidence gate. New/Seen candidates are only offered as low-stakes written or phrase recognition when recognition is selected; they do not receive automatic productive prompts. A listening obligation is only planned when listening is enabled and suitable audio is available. Writing does not satisfy an unavailable listening obligation.

The Repository folds evidence into eight separate dimensions: exposure, assisted recognition, reading recognition, listening recognition, productive retrieval, contextual production, grammatical understanding, and phrase comprehension. GoReview's exercise families are mapped to the applicable assessment dimensions in `packages/goreview/src/types.ts`; these remain separate from the FSRS card schedule. GoLearn exposure/help/reading events can update exposure or recognition dimensions. GoTell judged production can update productive retrieval or contextual production according to the event's response type and evidence. GoReview `CardReviewed`, `AssessmentAnswered`, and `ListeningAssessed` events likewise contribute only their supported dimensions and are filtered by their evidence quality and assistance. A correct exercise does not by itself assert global mastery.

`projection@7` derives New, Seen, Recognized, Familiar, Known, and Automatic. Familiar requires recognition score at least 0.6 across two observations. Known requires score at least 0.8, four observations, four independent positive events, and two delayed independent positives separated by at least 24 hours. Automatic is a learner mark or preserved legacy state; production counts alone do not promote it. A New mark is released by any later observation (at least Seen); earlier history does not undo the reset. Other explicit marks remain in force until the learner marks again. See [knowledge levels](ecosystem/KNOWLEDGE-LEVELS.md) and `packages/repository-engine/src/levels.ts` for thresholds and projection details.

Goal settings affect candidate selection and exercise choice, not the facts or grades. The client passes requested objective dimensions to the Repository candidate query so a primary reading objective does not hide a still-uncovered goal skill. The client then selects one supported objective at a time using candidate target/objective dimensions, practice goals, spaced independent recognition, card history, and exercise availability. Retrieval, application, and listening choices have separate gates. Productive response families are not multi-choice assessment: no multi-choice productive-evidence claim is made.

## Scheduling, queues, and workload

FSRS 6 (`ts-fsrs` 5.4.2) advances the review schedule from the recorded grade using the scheduler profile attached to the review. The shipped defaults use desired retention 0.9, short-term learning steps of 1 and 10 minutes, a 10-minute relearning step, and no fuzz so event replay is deterministic. Settings can alter supported retention/profile parameters. An unsupported profile is surfaced rather than guessed. See [scheduler behavior](../packages/goreview/src/scheduler/README.md).

The planner has four automatic queues, ordered as due learning/relearning, due reviews, eligible new admissions, and the separate remediation tier when explicitly selected for reassessment. Within comparable due queues, due time and priority policy order items. Explicit requested work can be kept in its requested order. Remediation is not silently inserted to fill scheduled practice.

The initial batch defaults to 10 questions and is bounded by the user's configured maximum (policy target maximum 20; the saved control can also be set to zero). It may adapt by two questions, within the 4–20 target bounds and the configured cap. The current provisional rules look at up to 30 eligible review observations from the last 90 days: at least six latency samples across two modalities are needed for a slow-response reduction, and expansion needs at least ten fast samples across two modalities. Slow responses are combined with low independent accuracy, repeated failure, or high candidate cognitive cost; speed alone is not sufficient. Low independent accuracy is below 0.62, repeated-failure rate is 0.3, slow is 1.4× estimated time, and fast is at most 0.65×. The planner shows its target, eligible work, estimated active-practice time, and configured workload limit. These heuristics are provisional and need real learner data before calibration.

Estimated durations are 25 seconds for written recognition/retrieval, 15 seconds for a new/learning multiple-choice recognition item, 35 seconds for grammar, 40 seconds for sentence completion, and 38 seconds for audio (30 seconds plus one expected 8-second replay). Measured review time can replace a per-item estimate when valid. The session time limit is a separate hard workload cap; zero means flexible time. The total question cap also includes timed returns. A return is admitted only if both caps allow it.

New admissions additionally obey the per-session `maxNew` and UTC-day `dailyNewLimit`. The daily allowance counts one automatic introduction per canonical scheduling card on its first creation/response date; explicit selected practice does not consume the automatic allowance. This is an event-replicated global allowance: offline devices can temporarily exceed the global daily cap, then reconcile when their event histories sync. Admission reserves estimated time for due work and upcoming short-term learning/relearning (and scheduled reviews inside the session window), then applies the configurable workload-aware policy; its current expected-return ratio defaults to 0.35. The fixed comparison policy disables only this workload-based throttle, not hard count/time caps.

## Timed returns and repeated presentations

FSRS learning/relearning due times are actual timestamps. If a short-term return becomes due during a session, the runner refreshes it from persisted events and inserts it at the next opportunity. It may displace a lower-priority unshown question; displaced work remains deferred for later planning. Completed answers are already persisted. The runner checks remaining presentation count and active-practice budget before inserting a return, and stores pending returns/deferred work so refresh or interruption does not erase them.

Each repeated presentation records a typed overlap (`scheduled`, `remediation`, or `explicit`) and a reason such as a due learning return, scheduled review, delayed remediation reassessment, or explicit repeat. The source of the label is planner/runner metadata persisted with the review event, not UI inference. Repeated questions are therefore distinguishable from the same item appearing in separate sessions.

## Remediation and completion

Remediation is a separate, evidence-derived backlog. Its labels name the failed target dimension/family (failed retrieval, productive retrieval, morphology, context, listening) and may add repeated failure only when repeated failures are actually observed. They do not diagnose learner confusion. A normal unresolved eligible failure can be offered as a study opportunity; the study marker records no grade or knowledge. After study, reassessment waits 10 minutes and is explicitly requested. Persistent failure (at least five Again grades in the recent ten effective eligible reviews) requires studying repair material and an independent delayed reassessment. Only evidence meeting the repair criteria resolves it. Invalid or challenged questions are not repairable by this path.

Session completion reports answered/shown/planned work separately. Continue choices distinguish ready scheduled work, pending short-term return, optional remediation, bounded optional repeats, and the GoLearn reading continuation. Ending early preserves completed reviews and leaves unanswered items due. Optional work does not change ordinary scheduled membership or justify extra due cards.

## Collection scopes

The ordinary GoReview queue contains eligible scheduled cards and justified intake. It does not fill a batch from the entire vocabulary catalog. Manually selected vocabulary has a separate explicit-practice route; saved decks retain their collection membership, while only eligible items within current deck/time limits enter a repeat session. “Selected” and “saved” indicate user collection state, not automatic next-batch membership.

Class Essentials has a separate, clearly labeled due-only session. It includes existing reviewed class cards only; unreviewed seeded phrases remain in the saved Class Essentials collection and are never counted as due. It uses the same eligibility, audio, scheduling and session limits while preserving class identity. Class Essentials failures can appear in remediation with a source label, but remain outside ordinary vocabulary scheduling. Provisional class facts do not currently support the strict verified-facts repair evidence required to clear persistent class-card repair cases or prove strong mastery; this limit is not bypassed.

## Compatibility and release verification

Legacy review histories are imported conservatively. Histories without safe event-level markers cannot always distinguish a legacy Hard grade from explicit learner intent; the system does not invent a self-mark or use that ambiguous history to bypass the current admission evidence gates. Unsupported scheduler profiles, conflicting IDs, and missing facts similarly fail closed for the affected path.

For local work, use `npm run release:preflight:local` to run typecheck/regressions and verify an explicitly developer-mode native payload. For release-source verification without Apple configuration or a service-health request, run `node packages/goreview-ios/scripts/release-preflight.mjs --web-only`; it requires a clean release-eligible checkout and writes its receipt outside the hashed artifact. Native preparation adds a linked parent GoReview manifest and a final transformed-payload manifest; `npm run release:preflight` additionally requires valid Bundle ID/service configuration and performs its configured health check. `release:stage` verifies the native payload before and after copying it into the standalone EAS stage, and staged validation checks it again. Details: [release traceability](goreview-release-traceability.md) and [iOS wrapper instructions](../packages/goreview-ios/README.md).

The manifests provide hashes and source-to-artifact linkage, not cryptographic signatures. No production submission, native archive, or TestFlight release is claimed by this documentation.
