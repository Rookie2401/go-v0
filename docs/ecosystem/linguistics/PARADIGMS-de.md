# German paradigms (Linguistic Engine V2, Stage 3)

`deAdapter.paradigm(lemma, pos, hint?)` returns the forms of a lexeme as feature-keyed cells; `deAdapter.featuresFromTableTags(tags, pos)`
maps Wiktionary (kaikki.org) table tags to the same keys; `deAdapter.cellsFor(paradigm, features, surface?)` projects an analysed reading
onto cells. `deParadigmFromTable` merges the two halves with `mergeParadigm` (verified / supported / generated / conflicted per cell).
Code: `src/adapters/german/paradigm.ts`, `src/adapters/german/table-tags.ts`. Probe: `scripts/probe-paradigm-de.mts`
(30 seconds for the whole 371,261-line dump). Result: `paradigm-de-2026-10-10.json`. Tests: `test/adapters/german-paradigm*.test.ts`.

Nothing is generated by a rule of its own. The cells are the forms the analysis already produces (`nounForms`, `adjectiveForms`,
`conjugateFull`, the closed-class table), with the analysis' features, so `cellOf` finds the cell of any analysed form. The word list is
machine-drafted and has had no native review.

## Inventory and layout

| Part of speech | `kind` | Cell features | Layout (columns / rows / sections) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NOUN | noun-declension | `Case` (Nom, Gen, Dat, Acc) × `Number`; an adjectival noun adds `Decl` | Number / Case / (Decl) |
| ADJ | adjective-declension | `Decl` (Strong, Weak, Mixed) × `Case` × `Gender` (singular) or plural (no Gender) × `Degree` (Cmp, Sup; none for the positive); the uninflected form is `Position: Pred` (+ Degree) | Gender (+ plural column) / Case / Degree, Decl |
| VERB | verb-conjugation | `VerbForm=Fin` × `Mood` (Ind, Sub, Imp) × `Tense` (Pres, Past; none for Imp) × `Person` × `Number`; `VerbForm=Inf` (+ `Variant: Zu`); `VerbForm=Part` + `Tense`; `Clause: Sub` on a separable verb's subordinate-clause form | Number / Person / VerbForm, (Clause), Mood, Tense, Variant |
| DET | determiner-paradigm | `Case` × `Gender` (singular) or plural | Gender (+ plural column) / Case |

Conventions:

- **Noun.** The cells hold the bare noun. Gender is a fact of the whole word and sits in the first note, which also gives the definite article of every
  cell (`dem Haus, ... / die Häuser, der Häuser, den Häusern`). The template is the dictionary header (`das Haus, des Hauses, die Häuser`, `(weak noun)`).
  Weak nouns, listed irregular forms, the second genitive (Mannes / Manns) and the older dative in -e (dem Hause) are extra forms of their cell
  (the first is the usual one). A noun listed under two genders (`See`) is chosen by `hint.features.Gender`; without it the first is shown and a note says so.
  An adjectival noun (die Deutsche) has no gender in the word list: the cells are the feminine singular and the plural in the three declensions.
- **Adjective.** 3 degrees × 3 declensions × 16 cells + 3 uninflected cells (gut, besser, am besten). The declined superlative has no uninflected form;
  `am besten` is built as `am` + the superlative stem + `-en`. An indeclinable adjective (rosa) has the 48 cells of the positive, all one form, and no comparison.
- **Verb.** Konjunktiv I is `Sub` + `Pres`, Konjunktiv II is `Sub` + `Past` (the analysis' convention). The imperative has Person 2 and no tense. The
  perfect, pluperfect, future and passives are `constructions` (`sentence-bracket-perfect`, `pluperfect`, `future`, `passive`, `passive-past`, `passive-perfect`;
  `separable-prefix-verb`, `reflexive-verb` when they apply), never cells; würde + infinitive and the polite imperative are not cells either. A separable
  verb's finite cells are the main-clause forms with the particle separated (`steht auf`, `stand auf`, `steh auf`) and, as `Clause: Sub` cells, the written-together
  forms (`aufsteht`); the notes name the particle and the perfect auxiliary. A reflexive verb's cells are the bare verb; a note says the pronoun accompanies it.
  `hint.features.Separable` ('Yes' / 'No') chooses between a separable and an inseparable verb of one spelling (umfahren).
- **Determiner.** `der`, `ein` (no plural), `kein`, the possessives, `dieser`-type words, from the closed-class table. A note names the adjective declension that
  follows (weak after der / dieser, mixed after ein / mein).
- **Unknown lemmas.** A noun or adjective is never guessed. A verb the list does not hold is generated only through the two explicit paths the analysis also
  has, and the first note starts "Guessed:" with no template: (1) a separable prefix + a listed verb (mit + fliegen → `flog mit`), using the base verb's principal
  parts; (2) a lower-case verb in -en / -ern / -eln with no verb prefix at the start, as a regular weak verb. A verb that starts with a separable or two-way
  prefix over a rest the list does not hold (aufwerten, umfärben) gets nothing.
- **`cellsFor` / `germanCellFeatures`.** A noun reading's Gender and Decl are dropped (the cells do not carry them); an adjective reading with no Case is the
  `Position: Pred` cell; a reading without Degree is the positive. A union value ("Nom,Acc", "Masc,Neut", Person "1,3") matches every cell it could be; the
  surface form picks between the main-clause and the subordinate-clause cell of a separable verb and between `gehen` and `zu gehen`.

## The table-tag mapper

Tag spellings were checked against the real file (not written from memory): `nominative / genitive / dative / accusative`, `singular / plural`, `masculine / feminine /
neuter`, `strong / weak / mixed`, `first-person / second-person / third-person`, `indicative`, `present`, `preterite`, `subjunctive` + `subjunctive-i` / `subjunctive-ii`,
`imperative`, `infinitive` + `infinitive-zu`, `participle` + `present` / `past`, `comparative`, `superlative`, `predicative`, `subordinate-clause`, and the context tags
`definite / indefinite / negative / without-article / includes-article`. A row names exactly one cell; a row with two values of a feature is not a cell.

Rows that map to nothing: the template header rows (`table-tags`, `inflection-template`), `class`, `auxiliary`, every `multiword-construction` row (perfect,
pluperfect, future: the constructions), the declined-participle rows that sit inside a verb entry, diminutives, a noun row with a Gender or Degree. An "includes-article"
form is compared without its article (`der große` → `große`, `keine großen` → `großen`); `-` is an empty cell; `older also: daß` is an annotation; the reflexive pronoun of a cited
infinitive (`sich sputen`) is dropped. Usage and region labels (archaic, proscribed, Austria ...) do not change the cell; the first two kinds go in the cell's note.
Konjunktiv II rows are tagged `formal rare` in the dump (the würde-form is the ordinary one) — they are still the Konjunktiv II cells.

The low noun and adjective coverage is the word list's size (about 2,900 entries), not a generation limit; "supported" is mostly cells of lemmas the adapter does not hold.
Verbs split into two very different groups:

- **word-list verbs: 843 lemmas, 30,803 cells verified, 77 conflicted (0.2 %)**;
- **guessed verbs: 6,726 lemmas, 208,589 verified, 18,727 conflicted (8.2 %)** — the weak guess is right for about 92 % of cells; it is wrong for a strong verb,
  and for separable verbs whose prefix the prefix list does not know (rum-, rad-, fehl-, spazieren-, runter-, raus- ...: the guess treats them as inseparable, so
  the zu-infinitive, the participle and every main-clause form disagree).

## Known disagreements

Found by the probe; none was fixed here (a fix changes the analysis output and needs an `adapter-de` version bump, which `packages/language-packs` and
`test/reader-languages/de.test.ts` pin, so they belong to the lane that owns the analysis).

1. **Superlative of adjectives in -end (analysis bug).** `superlativeOf` adds -est after -d, so *spannend* gets `am spannendesten`; German has `am spannendsten`
   (ansteckend, wütend, anstrengend, spannend: 49 conflicted cells each). The same wrong form is what the analysis recognises.
2. **Konjunktiv II of an inseparable strong verb with an unter- / über- front (analysis bug).** `umlaut()` takes the last umlautable vowel of the whole preterite,
   so *unterschied* → `ünterschiede` (should be `unterschiede`), *unterhielt* → `ünterhieltet`. Affects the inseparable verbs of the list with dual prefixes and no
   umlautable stem vowel.
3. **Word-list gaps that show as conflicts.** Verbs listed without principal parts although the dictionary has a strong homograph (verschieben → `verschiebtet` against `verschob`; bewegen, bereiten, reisen — the dictionary's rare strong verbs); verbs listed as plain that the dictionary treats as separable (radfahren, spazierengehen, hellsehen: zu-infinitive
   `radzufahren`); weak/strong homographs the list holds once (wiegen, schaffen, wachsen, hängen, erschrecken, weichen — one lexicon entry, two dictionary entries).
4. **Indeclinable colour words.** The word list marks rosa, lila as indeclinable; Wiktionary lists colloquial `rosaner`, `lilaner`. pink, super, leid, weh, untertan, orange,
   beige are declined by the dictionary and by the rules' regular endings differently (the list gives them no stem).
5. **Dictionary oddities** (not the rules' fault): `abstimme` for `abstimmte`, `angelest` for `angelesen`, `herüberkönnen` filed with `hinüberkönnen`'s forms.
6. **Dative singular in -e.** The table lists `Hause`; the rules too (older, literary): both are in the cell, verified.

## Not covered

- Lemmas outside the word list (except the verb guesses above); noun and adjective guesses; proper names (`PROPN`), pronouns (`PRON`), numerals.
- The polite imperative (`gehen Sie`), würde + infinitive, the periphrastic tenses and passives (constructions), the reflexive pronoun in the cells.
- Adjective declension of participles (the dictionary's verb entries carry them; they map to nothing), diminutives, comparison of adverbs.
- Gender of an adjectival noun other than the feminine / plural cells; the masculine and neuter singular are in the note only.
- Homographs of one spelling with the same gender and part of speech (weak/strong): the paradigm shows the first listed.
- Regional variants (Austria, Switzerland) and spelling variants (ß/ss, older spelling): a variant form in the table is an extra form of a cell, never its own cell.
