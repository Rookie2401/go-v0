# Latin grammar audit (Vulgate / Church Latin and classical Latin), 2026-11-17

Auditor: the Latin grammar-audit agent (an AI model, not a Latinist). Code audited: the shared clone at master `985c742b`
(read-only). Brief from the owner: audit the linguistic content, be brutal, note every missed feature and detail, and weigh
every finding against what the GoLearn card actually shows. Nothing was looked up: every judgement of Latin below is from my
own knowledge, and every quoted text was written out from memory.

## 1. Verdict

Latin is in a different position from Norwegian and from German. The word list is not a hand list: it is William Whitaker's
whole dictionary (plus Lewis & Short headwords and the Vulgate's names, 52,374 lexemes), with forms generated by rule from
the principal parts. So coverage is excellent and the lemma is usually right. What is wrong is the layer on top.

- **Coverage is not the problem.** On 1,110 words of running Latin (610 Vulgate and liturgy, 299 classical, 201 of my own)
  the card was blank for 0 words and guessed for 0. In 1,043 targeted probes it was blank 4 times.
- **The form line is the problem.** The analyser never uses the sentence to choose a case, number, tense or mood (its own
  notes say so), and the order it falls back on is a count of table cells. The result is systematic and wrong in the
  commonest words of the Vulgate: *Domini* in *Angelus Domini*, *verbum Domini*, *in domo Domini* is always "nominative or
  vocative plural"; *puellae* as subject is "dative or genitive singular"; *nomen tuum* is "accusative"; *Hoc est corpus
  meum* has *Hoc* "ablative"; *Quis es?* has *Quis* "ablative or dative plural"; *Is venit* has *Is* "ablative or dative
  plural"; *libera nos* has *nos* "nominative". I counted about 80 wrong readings in the 1,110 words (7 %), 8 of them in
  the 50 words of the Lord's Prayer, and a further third of all words carry a form line of the kind "ablative or nominative
  or vocative singular feminine" that the sentence decides.
- **Imperatives, greetings and adverbs lose to rarer words.** *Ora pro nobis* is "mouths", *Ave Maria* is "bird", *Salve*
  is the adjective "safe", *Ama Deum* is "bucket", *Sequere me* is "You will follow", *Veni, Creator* is "I came",
  *Tolle, lege* is "by law", *Libera nos* is the adjective "free"; *male*, *maxime*, *fortius*, *primum*, *multum*, *forte*
  are shown as adjectives.
- **The meaning line of verbs is often nonsense.** *es* ("you are") is "Be!"; *fit* is "He, she, or it becomes made";
  *licet* is "He, she, or it lawfuls"; *oportet* "necessaries"; *paenitet* "makeses angry"; *memini* "I remembered (PERF
  form"; *odi* "I hated"; *noli* "Be unwilling!".
- **The "All forms" table has a second source (Wiktionary tables, 54,221 records), but the comparison is broken.** The
  rules write no macrons, the dictionary does, and the two are compared letter for letter: *domini* against *dominī* is
  reported as a conflict. In 22 tables I built with the card's own merge function, 590 cells were "conflicted" and 263
  "verified". Real faults (*tribibus*, *cordum*) pass as verified because the right form is among the rules' variants.
- **The rules generate forms that do not exist**, and several rows are broken: the perfect of *utor* and of *audeo* cannot
  be read at all (*Usus est gladio*: "use", a noun; the table of *audeo* shows *aus sum, ai sumus, aurus*); 2,269
  adjectives get a comparative like *piior, idoneior, mediior*; every first- and second-declension noun gets a genitive
  plural in -um (*terrum, dominum, animum*); *davi, stavi, iuvui* are listed as perfects.
- **Grammar notes are thin for the Vulgate.** *quia / quoniam / quod* for reported speech, *dixit ad*, *coepit* +
  infinitive, *noli* + infinitive, conditionals, indirect questions: nothing. *quod* is always the relative pronoun
  (*Scio quod Deus bonus est*: "subject of Scio"). The pack's own example of an ablative absolute, *Deo duce*, gets no
  note, while *Alea iacta est* and *Gallia est omnis divisa* get one.

It is usable as a dictionary look-up with a lemma. As a teacher of Latin forms, which is what a Latin card is for, it is
not ready: a learner who believes the form line will learn that *Domini* is a plural.

## 2. How this was audited, and the numbers

1. Read the adapter: `la.ts`, and in `latin/` the files `lexicon`, `lexicon-load`, `lexicon-extra`, `morph-noun`,
   `morph-verb`, `verb-tables`, `morph-pronoun`, `morphology`, `orthography`, `guess`, `sentence`, `context`, `agreement`,
   `describe`, `word-analysis`, `priors`, `priors-vulgate`, `constructions`, `syntax`, `paradigm`, `pronunciation`,
   `types`, `version`; `docs/latin/ADAPTER.md`; the pack and the orientation; the card parser (`parser/index.ts`).
   Not read line by line: `closed-data.ts` (I read its tables through the dump), `gloss-data.ts`, `tags.ts`,
   `production.ts`.
2. **What the analyser is.** Seed list (1,924 lexemes: about 1,500 Whitaker rows ranked by Vulgate counts, the closed
   classes ported from the owner's Romance app, hand additions). On load the extension chunk calls `loadLatinLexicon()`,
   which adds Whitaker's whole DICTLINE, Lewis & Short headwords Whitaker lacks, and 146 Vulgate names: 50,450 more
   lexemes. Forms are generated by rule from the principal parts; a homograph is ranked by lemma count times a cell
   weight times a treebank prior (Perseus classical prose) and 35 hand-written "Vulgate priors". The sentence is used for
   five things only: what a preposition governs, *cum*, *quam*, *vis*, *mei/tui/nostrum*, and noun-phrase agreement (which
   may raise a reading but never narrows one). **Spelling expected:** none in particular. The lookup key folds case,
   ligatures (æ, œ, ę), every accent and length mark, j to i and v to u, so Clementine print (*cælum, ejus*), classical
   print, all-capital inscriptions (*POPVLVSQVE*) and liturgical accents (*Dóminus*) all reach the same entry. "Clementine
   Vulgate orthography" in the catalogue describes the gloss order and the priors, not a spelling restriction.
3. Dumped every generated form (`dump.mts`): read **all** seed rows (614 nouns, 134 names, 584 verbs, 169 adjectives) and
   the closed tables I printed; the 50,450 rows of the full lexicon were **not** read, only searched mechanically for the
   fault classes the seed showed (counts in section 5).
4. Checked stored principal parts of 187 common verbs and the genitive and gender of 164 common nouns against values I
   wrote from memory (`core.mts`).
5. Ran **1,043 word-in-sentence probes** in ten batteries through the card's own `parseSelection` and the dictionary
   `gloss` (with the full lexicon loaded, as in the extension), **8 more in the seed-only state**, and 64 + 29 single
   words through the pronunciation line. Three probes hit the wrong word (a one-letter target inside a longer word) and
   are ignored.
6. Ran **1,110 words of running text** word by word (`cover.mts`), then read the output and counted wrong readings by hand.
7. Built the "All forms" table for 22 lemmas with the card's own merge (`buildParadigm` in
   `packages/golearn-extension/src/content/enrich.ts`) against the built Wiktionary records (`merge.mts`).
8. Checked 458 everyday words against the list.

### 4.7 Spelling

- **Right:** u for v and v for u (*Uia, uillam, Vnus*), j (*Jam, Ejus, Jesus, Cujus, Major, Judicium*), ligatures

- **Variant spellings are separate lemmas.** *caelum*, *coelum* and *celum* are three lemmas; so are *caritas / charitas*,
  *cotidianus / quotidianus*, *littera / litera*, *sepulcrum / sepulchrum*, *paenitentia / poenitentia*, *gratia / gracia*,

  For a system that tracks which words a learner knows, one word is counted as two or three.

### 4.10 Role in the sentence

- It follows the form line, so it repeats its faults: *Puellae rosas amant*: "indirect object of amant"; *Illi
  dixerunt*: "indirect object"; *Romae habitat*: "indirect object of habitat"; *Liber puellae pulcher est*: "indirect
  object of est".
- A neuter is the subject by default: *Cor mundum crea in me*: "subject of crea"; *Quid facis?*: "subject of facis";
  *Id non credo*: "subject"; *Nihil habeo*: "subject"; *Bella gerunt Romani*: "subject of gerunt"; *Lac bibis?*:
  "subject of bibis".
- Place and time are objects: *Romam venit*: "direct object of venit"; *Domum redit*, *Martha domum it*: "direct object".
- With no finite verb the first noun becomes the head and the rest hang on it: *Senatus populusque Romanus*: "subject of
  Senatus"; *Deo gratias*: "direct object of Deo"; *Pater familias*: *familias* "accusative plural, direct object of
  Pater" (it is the old genitive singular); *Honoris causa*: "subject of Honoris"; *Homo homini lupus*: "indirect object
  of Homo"; *Homo magnae sapientiae*: "indirect object of Homo".
- A substantive adjective is "predicate adjective": *Omnia vincit amor*; *Senes consilium dant*; *Esurientes implevit
  bonis*; *Videns autem turbas*.
- An infinitive subject is "infinitive that completes the verb" (*Errare humanum est*).

- *fio* is labelled a deponent (*Fit, Fiat, Fiebant*). It is the opposite: active forms, passive meaning.
- "Perfect passive" on deponent perfects (4.9).
- *Michi placet*: "Spelling differs from today's standard (mihi)" is right; *Autor*: "(author)" is wrong (4.7).
- The orientation says "the card shows every reading instead of choosing one". The card shows one reading and up to three

## 5. The "All forms" table

### 5.1 How the table is built, and what goes wrong in the merge

Latin, unlike Norwegian and German, has a second source: `public/dict/la/full/` holds 54,221 Wiktionary records, 52,668
with inflection tables (with macrons). The card merges the adapter's cells with the dictionary's and marks each cell
verified, conflicted, rules-only or dictionary-only. I ran that merge (`buildParadigm`) for 22 lemmas; I did not see it
in a browser.

- **A macron is a conflict.** *dominus*: 9 of 12 cells conflicted ("shown dominī, rules gave domini"). *amo*: 99
  conflicted, 10 verified. Over the 22 lemmas: 590 conflicted, 263 verified, 647 rules-only, 218 dictionary-only. The
  status says nothing about whether the rules are right. On a conflict the dictionary form replaces the rule form, so the
  table is a mixture of forms with and without macrons.
- **A wrong main form passes as verified** when the right form is among the rules' variants: *tribus* dative plural
  (rules: *tribibus*, variant *tribubus*; dictionary: *tribubus*) and *cor* genitive plural (rules: *cordum*, variant
  *cordium*) are both "verified", and the rule form stays in the cell (the last point from the merge code).
- **Where the two truly differ the dictionary wins, and the rule form is kept as the alternative:** *pius* vocative
  shown *pie* (rules: *pi*); *meus* vocative shown *mī* (rules: *mee*); *vas* shown *vāsis, vāsa, vāsōrum* (rules:
  *vasi, vaso, vasum, vasos*); *castra* shown *castrōrum* (rules: *castrae, castrarum, castras*).
- **Where the dictionary has no matching cell the rule form stands alone:** *fides* plural *fiderum, fidebus*; *audeo*
  participles *aus, aa, aum, aurus, aura, aurum*; every comparative and superlative (*pius*: 78 rules-only cells
  including *piior*; *vetus*: 104 rules-only cells including *veterior, veterissimus*, beside 12 dictionary-only cells;
  the dictionary record gives *vetustissimus / veterrimus*: the two sources do not line up for third-declension
  adjectives of one ending);
  every two-word perfect passive.

### 5.2 Rule faults (from the dump; counts are over the whole lexicon unless stated)

1. **Deponents whose participle stem ends in -us lose their participle.** `perf.replace(/\s*us sum$/, '')` eats the
   stem: *audeo, ausus sum* gives *aus, aurus, aum esse, aus sum, ai sumus*; *utor, usus sum* gives no participle and no
   perfect at all; *abutor, coutor, deutor, adfundor, affundor* likewise (7 verbs). *deutor* then produces the forms
   *deus, deum, dei, deis* and attaches itself to the word God.
2. **A genitive plural in -um is generated for every first- and second-declension noun and adjective** as "a variant
   form": *terrum, animum, vium, dominum, populum, verbum, puerum, Christum, meum, bonum*. A few are real poetic forms
   (*deum, virum, divum, superum*); the rule makes them all. Each is also a reading of the accusative singular form.
3. **A genitive plural in -ium is generated as a variant for every consonant stem** (*regium, patrium, hominium,
   nominium, fratrium*) and one in -um for every i-stem (*gentum, urbum, noctum, marum*).
4. **Main form wrong, right form only a variant** (third and fourth declension): *cor*: *cordum* (cordium); *caro*:
   *carnum* (carnium); *venter*: *ventrum* (ventrium); *imber*: *imbrum* (imbrium); *nix*: *nivum* (nivium); *os, ossis*:
   *ossum* (ossium); *torrens*: *torrentum* (torrentium); *infans*: *infantum* (infantium); *par, paris* neuter: *pare,
   para, parum* (pari, paria, parium); *nectar*: *nectari, nectaria* (nectare); *iubar*: *iubari* (iubare). Fourth
   declension: *tribibus* (tribubus), *arcibus* (arcubus), *artibus* (artubus), *lacibus* (lacubus), *quercibus*
   (quercubus), *specibus* (specubus), *partibus* (partubus), *acibus* (acubus). *navis*: *navim* is the main accusative
   and *navem* the variant (the other way round).
5. **Fifth declension: a full plural for every noun** (73 of 73): *fiderum, fidebus; sperum, spebus; facierum,
   faciebus; meridierum*. Only *res* and *dies* have these cases; *fides* has no plural.
6. **Vocative of adjectives in -ius is -i** (1,653 adjectives): *pi, impi, egregi, medi, vari, propri, necessari*.
   Correct: *pie, impie, egregie*. (The -i vocative belongs to *filius*, *genius* and proper names. For common nouns such
   as *gladius, nuntius, socius, fluvius* the rule also gives *gladi, nunti*: check.) *meus*: *mee* (mi). *tuus, suus*:
   *tue, sue* (no vocative exists).
7. **Comparison is generated for almost every adjective.** The no-comparison list has 19 words. Results:
   - adjectives in -ius, -eus, -uus (2,269): *piior, idoneior, mediior, aureior, dubiior, arduior, vacuior, perpetuior,
     necessariior, egregiior*; superlatives *mediissimus, aureissimus* (these adjectives compare with magis / maxime;
     *piissimus* is late Latin);
   - irregular ones: *pauperissimus* (pauperrimus), *veterior, veterissimus* (vetustior, veterrimus), *iuvenior,
     iuvenissimus* (iunior), *senissimus*, *dexterrimus* (dextimus), *exterrimus* (extremus), *inferissimus* (infimus),
     *superissimus* (supremus, summus), *posterissimus* (postremus), *degenerissimus*;
   - *-ficus, -dicus, -volus*: *magnificior* beside the right *magnificentior*; *maledicior, benevolior, munificior,
     mirificior* alone (maledicentior, benevolentior);
   - words with no degrees: *universior, cunctior, singulior, aeternior, mortuior, primogenitior, anniculior,
     Chananeior, futurior, talior, qualior, tantior, quantior, novissimior, novissimissimus*.
   The comparative and superlative of the plain adverbs (*diu, saepe*) are, on the other hand, not generated (4.1).
8. **Future participle built on the supine only:** *mortuurus* (moriturus; shown in the table of the seed row *morior,
   mori, mortuus sum* with *mortuurum esse* and the supine *mortuu*), *naturus* (nasciturus), *orturus* (oriturus),
   *parturus* (pariturus), *ruturus* (ruiturus), *iuturus* (iuvaturus), *secturus* (secaturus), *arguturus*
   (arguiturus), *fructurus* (fruiturus).
9. **Invented perfects, listed as variants:** a regular -avi perfect for every first-conjugation verb with an irregular
   one (92 verbs): *davi, davit, daverunt, davisse* (do), *stavi* (sto), *circumdavi, constavi, instavi*;
   and a -ui perfect for roots in -iuv: *iuvui, adiuvui*. *contrii* (contrivi) by the rule that drops v from every
   perfect in -ivi.
10. **Imperatives.** *dice, duce, face* stand first, with *dic, duc, fac* added beside them; for the compounds of
    *facio* in -facio (84 verbs) only *beneface, satisface, caleface, pateface* exist in the table (benefac, satisfac …).
11. **Passive forms for a semi-deponent** (*auderi, auderier*); an archaic infinitive in -ier and a gerundive in -undus
    for every verb (*amarier, dicier, comburundus*): harmless as variants, noise in a learner's table.
12. *hic*: the particle -ce is added to every form ending in c or s: *hicce, huicce, huncce, hancce, hocce, haecce,
    hacce* beside the real *huiusce, hosce, hasce, hisce* (check: *hunce, hance* are the attested forms).
13. *alius*: accusative and vocative neuter *alium* (aliud). *unus*: the table has ten cells (no *unae, unis, unos,
    unas, unorum*; these are read through a second lexeme). *eo*: future imperative *ito* only for the second person,
    *eunto*; no gerundive.

## 6. Grammar not modelled at all

- **Choice of case, number, tense or mood from the sentence**: subject and verb agreement, a genitive after its noun, a
  neuter plural with *sunt*, an object of a transitive verb. This is the first gap and the adapter states it.
- **The locative**, except *domi*: *Romae habitat* (dative or genitive, "indirect object"), *Ruri vivit* (dative),
  *Humi iacet* (nominative plural), *Athenis*, *Domi militiaeque*. Accusative of motion and ablative of place from
  which (*Romam venit; Roma profectus est*: "subject of profectus").
- **The imperative as a sentence type**: no use of sentence-initial position, of a vocative beside it, or of "!".
- **Adverbs of adjectives in the comparative and superlative**, and the comparison of *diu, saepe, prope*.
- **quod / quia / quoniam** as conjunctions against the relative; **quo, qua, quam, quando** as interrogatives; *cum*
  before a nominative.
- **Vowel length**, even when the text prints it.
- **Constructions** listed in 4.9 with "no note": Vulgate reported speech, *dixit ad*, *coepit* + infinitive, the
  nominative participle, prohibitions, conditions, indirect questions, relative clauses with the subjunctive, *quin*,
  *utinam*, *dum / priusquam*, the datives and ablatives by name, the supine of purpose, the impersonal passive.
- **Sequence of tenses; the historic present; the historic infinitive; indirect speech beyond one infinitive.**
- **Roman numerals in lower case, Roman numerals with a value on the card, dates** (*a.d. III Kal. Ian.*), abbreviations
  beyond a short list, liturgical signs.
- **Verse**: elision, scansion, poetic forms (*divom, superum* as genitives, *-ere* is covered).
- **The enclitic -ne shortened to -n, -ce, -met** beyond pronouns (met is covered for pronouns; not probed).
- **A frequency list** for the dictionary.

## 7. Orientation and course texts

**Orientation (`tutorial/la.ts`).**

- "When you select a word, the card shows its case: Nom for the one who acts, Acc for the one acted on." True for
  *puer* and *puellam*. Not true for most words: the card shows a list or the wrong single value (4.2, 4.3). "When the
  ending of a word could belong to more than one case, the card shows every reading instead of choosing one" is not what
  the card does either.
- The demo passage, sentence 6, *Postea Marcus et Iulia domum laeti ambulant*: the card shows *laeti* as "genitive
  singular masculine or neuter" and *domum* as "direct object of ambulant". Sentence 3, the practice target *magna*:
  "ablative or nominative or vocative singular feminine" at 0.63. Sentence 4, *librum*: right once the full lexicon has
  loaded, a guess before.
- "Latin is written … with no accents": liturgical books print them, and the pack reads them.
- "h is silent" (Church Latin) is given without the exception the pronunciation note itself prints (*mihi, nihil*).
- The Latin of the passage is correct as far as I can judge. *amatis* "you (all) love" and the other glosses are right.

**Stage 0 and the ladder** (I read scenes 1, 8, 15, exit A and ladder units 1 to 5 and the start of 6, of 12; under half of the material).

- The Latin is simple and I found no wrong form in what I read.
- **Present tense with *cras*** throughout, by declared house style: *Cras panem emis; Cras ad forum eo; Cras in foro
  sum; Cras ad ecclesiam imus; Cras Marthae gratias ago*. Latin wants the future there (*emes, ibo, ero, ibimus*). The
  course is teaching a habit it will have to unteach.
- *Samuel in mensa epistulam scribit*, glossed "at the table": *in mensa* is "on the table" (*ad mensam*).
- *Invenio!* glossed "I find it" where a speaker says *Inveni* ("I have found it"). *nutrix* for a hospital nurse is a
  modern stretch of "wet-nurse" (check).
- **The card misreads the course's own sentences** (battery 12): *Quis es?* (Stage 0, scene 1): *Quis* "ablative or
  dative plural"; *Salve, Martha!*: adjective "safe"; *Cras ad forum eo*: *eo* the pronoun; *Pueri et puellae in schola
  sunt*: *puellae* "dative or genitive singular, indirect object of sunt"; *Domus tua et domus mea bonae sunt*: *bonae*
  "dative or genitive singular"; *Estisne fessi? / Non sumus fessi*: "genitive singular"; *Hodie multi in valetudinario
  sunt*: "genitive singular"; *Multa habes*: "ablative or nominative or vocative singular feminine"; *Clavis tua hic
  est*: *hic* the pronoun; *Tu amica bona es*: *es* "Be!"; *Caput meum dolet*: *meum* "accusative"; *Lac bibis?*:
  "subject of bibis".

## 8. What to fix first

1. **Rank readings with the sentence** for the six cases that cause most of the damage: (a) a form in -i or -ae directly
   after a noun is a genitive; (b) a nominative-plural reading wins when the clause verb is plural and no other subject
   stands; (c) -um, -a and -ia beside a neuter noun or with *est / sunt* take the neuter nominative; (d) *nos, vos* with a
   verb of another person are accusative; (e) *is, quis, quisque, quisquam, hoc* take the nominative before the rare
   *is = iis* and *quis = quibus*; (f) a participle or adjective in -i with *sunt, sumus, estis* is nominative plural.
2. **Fix the participle of *utor, audeo* and the five others** (one regular expression), which also removes *deutor*
   from every card of *Deus*.
3. **Imperatives**: sentence-initial or after a vocative, before "!" or a comma; drop or reverse the priors `ora → os`
   and `sine`; add *ave, salve, vale* as greetings; give deponent *-re* the imperative first when no subject *tu* stands.
4. **The meaning line**: *es* "you are"; *fio* "become, happen, be made" and not a deponent; impersonals from a hand
   list (*licet* "it is permitted" …); *memini, odi* as presents; *noli(te)* + infinitive "do not"; present before
   perfect for *venit / fugit* unless a past adverb stands; "we / us" for *nos*.
5. **The table merge**: compare with macrons stripped; let the dictionary form stand when the rules' main form differs
   and only a variant matches; stop generating the genitive plural in -um and -ium for all nouns, the plural of the fifth
   declension, comparison of adjectives in -ius / -eus / -uus, the -avi and -ui "variants", *dice / duce / face*; take
   irregular superlatives and future participles from a short hand list.
6. **Wrong rows** of 5.3 (*vas, libamen, castra, eicio, comburo, disperdo, adoleo, depono, veto, sero, edo, liber,
   liberi*, the plural-only genitives, *azyma, sartatecta*), and add *neuter*, *decies / quinquies*, *Genitrix*,
   *eleison*, *milia* as the plural of *mille* ahead of *milium*.
7. **Adverbs**: an -e / -ius / -issime form with no noun to agree with is the adverb; generate *diutius, saepius,
   saepissime*; *primum, multum, tantum, solum, forte, modo* by position.
8. ***quod, quia, quoniam*** after verbs of saying and knowing: the conjunction, with a Vulgate note; *cum* + nominative
   + verb: the conjunction; *vis* at the head of a clause with a genitive after it: the noun.
9. **Names at the start of a sentence** when the word list has a name of that spelling and the alternative is rare
   (*Maria, Paulus, Marce*); join spelling variants (*caelum / coelum / celum*) to one lemma.
10. **Grammar notes**: ablative absolute for noun + noun and pronoun + adjective, and not for a nominative with *est*;
    gerund against gerundive (*amandi* with no noun is a gerund); no "perfect passive" on a deponent; add the Vulgate
    constructions and the locative.
11. **Pronunciation**: consonantal i and u, the diphthongs *ui, eu*, the non-diphthongs (*poeta, aer, Israel*); the h
    note only for a free h; the v note for v; stress from the dictionary's macrons, and from "vowel before vowel" without
    them.
12. **Phrases**: the classical two-word units of 4.11.

## 9. Limits of this audit

- I am a language model, not a Latinist, and looked nothing up. Forms marked "check" are the ones I am not sure of. The
  texts in `text.txt` are from memory; a word may differ from the printed Clementine, Caesar, Cicero, Vergil or
  Catullus, and my own Latin (201 words) is a machine's.
- The full lexicon (50,450 rows) was searched for fault classes, not read. More wrong rows of the *vas / eicio* kind
  certainly exist there; my check of 187 verbs and 164 nouns found the ones listed and is a sample.
- The table merge was run through the extension's function, not seen in a browser. I do not know how the panel displays
  a "conflicted" cell. The same holds for the alternatives list and the raw `NumValue` feature.
- I did not measure how long the seed-only window lasts in the extension.
- Hand counts of wrong readings (about 80 in 1,110 words) are my own reading of the output. I counted a list that
  contains the right value as "undecided", not as wrong, and a single wrong value, or a list that leaves the right value
  out, as wrong.
- Not audited: `production.ts` (the checks on a learner's own Latin), `gloss-data.ts` beyond what the probes showed,
  ladder units 6 (most of it) to 12, Stage 0 scenes other than 1, 8, 15 and exit A, the sound scenes, and the evaluation figures of
  `docs/latin/EVAL.md` (I did not re-run them; my running-text counts are small beside them and measure the form line,
  which those figures score leniently).
- Medieval and Neo-Latin beyond a dozen spellings, verse beyond 11 lines, and inscriptions were barely tested.
