## What each comparison can and cannot show

**missing (the dictionary has no entry).** Can show: the dictionary file has no entry with a meaning of its own for that headword (an entry that only says "form of X" or "alternative spelling of X" does not count). Cannot show: that the word list is at fault or that the word is not Spanish; Wiktionary may list the word under another spelling, may call it a form of another word, or may simply lack it. Proper names are compared for presence only.

**part of speech.** Can show: none of the dictionary's parts of speech for the headword is the word list's, even after the equivalence table above. Cannot show which side is right: the two tag sets divide Spanish words differently, and a word that is a noun in one source and an adjective in the other (a substantivised adjective) is often not a mistake on either side. When this record is made the other comparisons for that entry are skipped.

**gender.** Can show: for a noun the word list gives a gender, the dictionary's gender (read from the masculine / feminine tags on the senses of every noun entry of the headword, form-of senses included, else from the first argument of the head template, whichever is present; the report counts which was used) shares none with it. A word used with both genders, written mf here or "by personal gender" there, is compatible with either. Cannot show: that a gender is wrong. A spelling shared by two nouns (el cura, la cura) looks like a disagreement because the entries are read together; a dictionary that gives no gender is skipped, not counted as agreement.

**meaning.** Can show: no content word of the word-list gloss appears in any gloss of the headword under a compatible part of speech. Cannot show that the meanings differ. This test is weak: a synonym, a paraphrase or a narrower sense looks exactly like a different meaning, and a gloss that happens to share a word with an unrelated sense passes. It is lenient on purpose: the gloss is tried as written, then in American spelling (centre / center), then for any whole word in common. A sense the dictionary words only as a form of another word (tía as "female equivalent of tío") has no English meaning in it, so such a word can be recorded here although the dictionary knows the meaning under the other word; the record then names that word. The comparison reads the first six glosses of each of the first 80 senses.

**variety (region).** Can show: the word list places a word in a region (Honduras, Central America, Latin America, Spain, Mexico, the Caribbean, the Andes, the Río de la Plata) and the dictionary's region labels on the word's senses name no place inside or around it; or the word list says Honduras or Central America and the dictionary gives no region label at all (recorded as weaker, with that note). Both sides are quoted verbatim. Cannot show: that the word is not used in the region. A label records where Wiktionary's editors know a sense to be used; most senses carry none, and a missing label is not evidence against a regional word. The labels are read for the whole word under a compatible part of speech (form-of senses included, so a word the dictionary has only as "female equivalent of ..." is still compared), not sense by sense: a label on an unrelated meaning of the same spelling counts, in both directions (the note of a record says when none of the labelled senses shares a word with the word-list gloss). A region the word list names outside Honduras and Central America with no dictionary label is not recorded (counted as not comparable). The region table above is a judgment of which labels "inside or around" a region.

**inflection (listed forms).** Can show: for an entry whose forms the word list writes out itself (irregular paradigms, the closed classes), a listed form that the dictionary neither has as a form-of entry for the lemma nor lists in the lemma's own table of forms. Cannot show that the form is wrong: the dictionary may file it under another lemma, spell it differently or omit a rare form (the future subjunctive, for example). The grammatical tags written in the word list are not compared, only the existence of the form.

All of them compare the word list with the dictionary as this tool reads it. A sense the tool does not read, a gloss beyond the caps or a region label not in the table above is invisible here.
