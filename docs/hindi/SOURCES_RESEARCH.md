# Hindi sources: rights and terms research

> **Machine-gathered research, not a rights determination. Nothing in this file authorises bundling, redistributing, or ingesting any text, audio, image, or data set.** Every row below records what a primary page said on the access date, nothing more. Where a page could not be reached, that is stated. A human rights reviewer (ideally with Indian and US copyright knowledge) must decide before any content ships.

- Access date for every item: **2026-10-01**.
- Method: pages were read (web fetch, a browser pane, plain HTTP GET of HTML/README/LICENSE files, the MediaWiki read API for Wikisource wikitext, GitHub's contents API). Files were **not** downloaded; sizes come from HTTP HEAD (`Content-Length`) or from the distributor's own listing. Exceptions, disclosed for honesty: (a) the first ~1.5 KB of `BibleAquifer/IndianRevisedVersion/hin/metadata.json` (a ~1 MB file) was read to see its licence block; (b) one StoryWeaver internal search-API call made by me returned HTTP 500 and was not repeated; (c) the first 600 bytes of StoryWeaver's `sitemap_hi.xml` were read to confirm it is an XML sitemap.
- Quotes are short and verbatim. Hindi quotes were copied from the page text; English quotes keep the source's own spelling.
- Not signed in anywhere, no terms accepted, no forms submitted. A cookie banner on digitallibrary.io and StoryWeaver's "Agree" bar were left untouched. A bot-check on wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de was not bypassed (see section 5).

### What a human rights reviewer still has to decide (section 1)

1. Whether the site-wide statement "all stories and illustrations CC BY 4.0" is enough, given the Terms' "or a third party" wording and the s.9D "Documents ... no modifications" paragraph that reads narrower than CC BY.
2. Whether to treat Readalong/audio as **excluded** (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0), which also bars derivative use such as sentence-level audio slicing.
3. How to carry per-story attribution (author, illustrator, translator, publisher, donor, year, source URL) in the app for every story, and what to do for translations "based on original story" (two layers of credit).
4. Whether automated retrieval is acceptable without permission, given no clause either way on StoryWeaver but an explicit prohibition on its sister site; the safe route is the written partner contact above.
5. Illustrations: CC BY, but `robots.txt` disallows `/illustrations/`.

### 2b. Is it CC BY-SA 4.0? Rights holder? Attribution? No-changes restriction?

- **Licence:** CC BY-SA 4.0 per eBible.org, YouVersion, Door43's manifest, and the Aquifer repo. (A CC "Attribution Share-Alike 4.0" wording; the eBible "Creative Commons Attribution Share-Alike license 4.0".) Confirmed on four distributor pages.
- **Rights holder named:** "Bridge Connectivity Solutions" (eBible, Aquifer, YouVersion publisher "Bridge Connectivity Solutions Pvt. Ltd."). The copyright years differ by distributor: 2017, 2018, 2019 (eBible) vs 2019 (Aquifer). The Door43 manifest names "Door43 World Missions Community" as `creator` with `publisher: 'BCS'`.
- **Attribution wording:** eBible asks only "You include the above copyright and source information." No fixed sentence. Door43 asks for the sentence "Original work available at https://door43.org/." for its own copy.
- **"No changes" restriction:** none. eBible expressly grants "reasonable revisions and adaptations", conditioned on marking changes and not implying endorsement, plus share-alike. It adds a non-legal note: "revising and adapting God's Word involves a great responsibility to be true to God's Word. See Revelation 22:18-19." (YouVersion has the same note.)
- **Provenance flag:** Door43's manifest lists a source `hbh` (version 1950) under `language: 'hi'`, i.e. IRV was produced as a revision of an earlier Hindi Bible. The eBible page itself does not state what IRV was revised from.
