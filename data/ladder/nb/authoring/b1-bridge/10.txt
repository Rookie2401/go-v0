=== nb-b1-bridge-10 | story | community | The neighbours | Naboene | neighbours,house meeting,rubbish,rules,relative clauses
speakers: solveig=Solveig, mons=Mons, sofie=Sofie
note: Relative clauses with "som", imperatives in the rubbish rules, "hvis" and "fordi" clauses. A house meeting in a Norwegian block of flats; the rules are invented and general. Names Mons and Sofie are invented.
## Solveig
Den første uka i den nye leiligheten hørte {sam} ofte Solveig under seg. Hun gikk i stua og sang. || The first week in the new flat Sam often heard Solveig below him. She walked in the living room and sang.
På onsdag kom hun til døren hans. «Jeg har laget kaffe,» sa hun. «Kom og drikk kaffe.» || On Wednesday she came to his door. "I have made coffee," she said. "Come and have coffee."
Det var mange bøker i leiligheten til Solveig, og {sam} satt i sofaen og drakk kaffe mens hun fortalte om kirka. || There were many books in Solveig's flat, and Sam sat on the sofa and drank coffee while she told about the church.
[solveig] Jeg har bodd i dette huset i femten år. Jeg kjenner alle som bor her. Du skal få treffe dem. || I have lived in this house for fifteen years. I know everyone who lives here. You shall get to meet them.

## Lappen
På torsdag var det en lapp på døren. Lappen sa: «Møte i huset på torsdag klokka sju. Alle må komme. Vi snakker om søppel og støy.» || On Thursday there was a note on the door. The note said: "Meeting in the house on Thursday at seven o'clock. Everyone must come. We will talk about rubbish and noise."
[sam] Hva er et møte i huset? || What is a meeting in the house?
[solveig] Det er et møte der alle som bor i huset treffes. Vi snakker om det som er viktig for oss alle. || It is a meeting where everyone who lives in the house meets. We talk about what is important for all of us.

## Møtet
Møtet var i et rom i huset, og det kom ti folk. En eldre mann som het Mons, åpnet møtet. || The meeting was in a room in the house, and ten people came. An older man called Mons opened the meeting.
[mons] God kveld. Vi har et problem med søppel. Noen kaster glass sammen med papir, og noen kaster plast i feil pose. || Good evening. We have a problem with rubbish. Someone throws glass together with paper, and someone throws plastic in the wrong bag.
[sofie] Jeg tror at det er barna mine som gjør feil. De er små, og de forstår ikke reglene. || I think it is my children who make mistakes. They are small, and they do not understand the rules.
[mons] Da må du vise dem. Papir og plast skal i hver sin pose, og glass skal i en kasse. Ikke kast glass i posene! || Then you must show them. Paper and plastic go in a bag each, and glass goes in a box. Do not throw glass in the bags!
[sofie] Jeg vet at søppel er et problem for barn. Men jeg skal skrive det på en lapp til dem: papir, plast og glass. || I know that rubbish is a problem for children. But I will write it on a note for them: paper, plastic and glass.
[solveig] Og {sam}, som er ny her, skal også få vite reglene. Alle har lest dem, men ingen husker dem. || And Sam, who is new here, shall also get to know the rules. Everyone has read them, but nobody remembers them.
[sam] Jeg skal lese dem i kveld. Men hva med støyen? || I will read them this evening. But what about the noise?
[mons] Hvis du skal ha besøk, er det bra før klokka ti. Etter klokka ti skal det være rolig. Barn lager ofte støy, og støy er også et problem. || If you are going to have visitors, it is fine before ten o'clock. After ten o'clock it must be quiet. Children often make noise, and noise is also a problem.
[sofie] Jeg er lei for det. Jeg skal snakke med barna. || I am sorry about that. I will talk to the children.

## Turene
Til slutt snakket de om å vaske trappen. Hver familie skulle vaske trappen en uke, og {sam} fikk den første uka i måneden. || At the end they talked about washing the stairs. Every family was to wash the stairs for a week, and Sam got the first week of the month.
[mons] Det er ikke vanskelig. Du trenger vann og litt tid. Men du må vaske hver dag. || It is not difficult. You need water and a little time. But you must wash every day.
{sam} lo. «Jeg har vasket gulvet hos {ana}, så jeg kan det,» sa han. || Sam laughed. "I have washed the floor at Anna's, so I know how," he said.
Da møtet var slutt, hilste Mons på ham. «Velkommen til huset,» sa han. «Du er en god nabo.» || When the meeting was over, Mons greeted him. "Welcome to the house," he said. "You are a good neighbour."

Den kvelden leste {sam} reglene en gang til. Han tenkte på hvor mye han hadde lært om å bo i Norge: regler, møter, dugnad og kaffe hos naboen. || That evening Sam read the rules once more. He thought about how much he had learned about living in Norway: rules, meetings, dugnad and coffee at the neighbour's.
Han skrev også et lite brev til {ana}: «Jeg har fått mange naboer. Jeg er ikke alene.» Så ba han Gud om å hjelpe dem alle, også Mons, og Sofie og barna. || He also wrote a little letter to Anna: "I have got many neighbours. I am not alone." Then he asked God to help them all, also Mons, and Sofie and the children.

Mons viste {sam} hvor han hadde gjort en feil, og han forklarte reglene en gang til. Han ga ham nummeret sitt og sa at {sam} kunne ringe hvis noe måtte males. {sam} hadde sin egen konto i banken, men han hadde ikke betalt regningen for søppelet ennå. || Mons showed Sam where he had made a mistake, and he explained the rules once more. He gave him his number and said that Sam could call if something had to be painted. Sam had his own account at the bank, but he had not yet paid the bill for the rubbish.
Solveig bar en tung pose i trappen, og {sam} hjalp henne. Han sammenlignet huset med det han hadde sett på visningen, og han tenkte at dette var mye bedre. Dagen etter gikk han i banken. || Solveig carried a heavy bag on the stairs, and Sam helped her. He compared the house with what he had seen at the viewing, and he thought that this was much better. The next day he went to the bank.
Mons spurte om han hadde skrevet under kontrakten. {sam} sa ja, og han fortalte om dugnaden da han flyttet. || Mons asked whether he had signed the contract. Sam said yes, and he told about the dugnad when he moved.
