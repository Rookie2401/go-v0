## Source, licence and split discipline

- **Treebank**: Universal Dependencies Korean-Kaist (UD_Korean-Kaist), generated by Chun et al. (2018) from the KAIST Tree-Tagging Corpus; CC BY-SA 4.0; the files are `data/raw/ud-ko/ko_kaist-ud-{train,dev,test}.conllu` of the main repository (README.md and LICENSE.txt beside them). Genre per its README: news, fiction, academic. Annotation: lemmas, UPOS and XPOS "converted from manual"; XPOS is the KAIST tag set, LEMMA the morphemes of the 어절 joined by `+`.
- The treebank is used **only to measure**. Nothing from it is copied into the product, and it is not a source for any lexicon row.
- **dev** is the split the builders tune against (this report). **test** is run ONCE, by the coordinator, at the end, with `--split test --final`; the script refuses `--split test` without `--final` before it opens any file, and it has not been run: **no figure in this report is a test figure**.
- **train** was used for counts by the lexicon build (the frequency prior). It is never scored here: no figure is measured on train. The only thing this script reads from the train file is the set of its FORM strings (column 2), to split dev 어절 into "form seen in train" and "form not seen in train".
- **Favourable to the lexicon**: the lexicon's counts came from train, which is the same treebank, hence the same newspaper, fiction and academic vocabulary as dev. Text from other domains will score lower than these figures. The "not seen in train" column is the closer guide, and even it shares the treebank's vocabulary and style.

## What was measured, and how

Every figure below is `count / total (percent)`, over the SCORED 어절 of the stated split and mode, one count per running token (not per distinct form). Percentages are rounded to two decimals, never up to a nicer number.

**Gold.** From each token line the gold LEXICAL lemma and class of the 어절's head are derived (the first content morpheme; a predicate stem tag pvg/pvd/paa/pad/px gives stem + 다; a noun followed by a derivational xsv/xsm (하, 되, 시키, 받, 당하, 스럽, 롭, 답) is the derived predicate noun+하다 and so on; a run of nouns is one compound; a leading numeral run is one numeral; an xp prefix is part of the headword; noun + xsa 히 is the adverb). The rest of the 어절 is the gold CHAIN of particles and endings. **System.** The analyser's FIRST reading is the one judged for "first"; "any" asks whether any reading offers the right headword.

**Lemma comparison** is on bare headwords (`koBareLemma`): a numbered homonym (눈²) or the undecided bare lemma (눈) both count as right when the headword is right. The first reading's homonym state is counted separately.

**Strict vs lenient.** The annotation scheme and a dictionary-based analyser legitimately cut some words differently (a compound the lexicon holds whole, a noun + 적 the lexicon holds as one headword, a noun + 하다 the lexicon has no headword for). STRICT accepts only the primary gold headword defined above. LENIENT also accepts the alternatives the annotation allows: the parts of a compound, the base noun before 적/씩/…, the base noun of a derived 하다/되다 verb, the unprefixed word after a prefix, the adverb's base. Every headline is given both ways.

## (d) Segmentation: particles and endings

Compared on the CHAIN after the head: gold = the morphemes after the head in the gold segmentation (derivational 하/되/시키/… and the parts of a compound belong to the head); system = the first reading's parts after its stem (a light-verb part and the nominal components of a compound belong to the head). Both sides are NORMALISED by `normMorph`: hyphens and optional (으)/(이) removed; allomorphs folded (은/는/ㄴ → ㄴ, 을/를/ㄹ → ㄹ, 이/가 → 이, 과/와 → 과, (으)로 → 로, 았/었/였/ㅆ → 었, a leading 아/여 → 어 (아서 = 어서), ㅂ니다/습니다 → 습니다, 는다 → ㄴ다, a leading 으 before a consonant syllable removed (으면 = 면), a leading 이 of a particle that has a vowel-final form removed (이나 = 나), 음/ㅁ → ㅁ, 에요 → 어요). The class of the morpheme is NOT compared (the copula 이 and the subject particle 이 are both "이"); what is compared is which particles and endings the first reading names, in order.

Comparable 어절: 21288 (gold chain texts known and a first reading present); not comparable: 657 (the lemma column holds only the stem, for an 어절 that starts with an auxiliary or the copula), plus those with no reading.

Further differences that are not counted by token: (1) KAIST distinguishes jcs / jcc / jco (subject, complement, object) and jxc/jxt (auxiliary, topic) particles; the comparison uses the morpheme text only, so a case label is never judged. (2) KAIST fuses some pre-final endings into one morpheme (었겠, 어야겠, ㄴ다거나); the system lists them apart; the "letters match" figure ignores this. (3) UD gives the copula 어절 (서울입니다) the UPOS VERB; the shared part of speech here is the head noun's, with the copula in the chain. (4) The UD UPOS of an mma word is ADJ; the head tag mapping above calls it DET (the pack's PLAN table). (5) KAIST writes ㄴ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ니다 as separate jamo morphemes; the system writes them the same way (-ㄴ, -ㄹ) but where it fuses a syllable the spans overlap.

## What this does not measure

- **Domains.** The treebank is written, edited, mostly 1990s-style expository prose, news and literature (and some academic text). It does NOT represent: speech or transcripts; the polite 해요체 forms a learner meets first (the treebank is overwhelmingly 하다체/해라체 narration and 합쇼체 in a few fiction passages, so 해요체 is almost absent); web text, chat, slang, neologisms, abbreviations; Bible prose and literary or archaic endings (-느니라, -나이다); Sino-Korean words in Hanja; children's text; dialogue-heavy modern fiction; text with numbers and Latin script (those 어절 are excluded here).
- **Favourable to the lexicon**: its counts come from train of the same treebank. Other text will score lower; the "not seen in train" column is the nearer guess, not a bound.
- **Meaning.** Only headword, shared part of speech and the particle/ending chain are compared. Nothing here says the gloss, the homonym choice, the features (tense, honorific, speech level, case), the constructions, the pronunciation or the phrase layer are right.
- **One gold reading per 어절.** The annotation gives one analysis; where a form is genuinely ambiguous the first reading may be a right answer the gold does not choose. "Any reading" recall measures the room the analyser leaves, not its choice.
- **Excluded 어절** (punctuation, symbols, foreign words, numbers in digits, bare particles, Hanja lemmas) are not scored; the analyser's behaviour on them (the digit + counter case 1445년, Latin + particle KBS가) is not measured here.
- **Head only.** Where an 어절 holds a second content word (a compound the lexicon reads as parts, an auxiliary 해보았다), only the first head is judged; the rest enters the chain comparison as content items.
- The test split has NOT been run.
